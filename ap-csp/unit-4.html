Computer Systems and Networks

How the internet moves data, why it keeps working when parts of it fail, and how splitting work across processors speeds things up. Three topics, dense with vocabulary.

What the College Board expects you to walk away understanding

  • The internet is a network of networks that moves data in packets using shared protocols, and it's designed so no single failure takes it down.
  • Fault tolerance comes from redundancy — multiple paths and backups.
  • Parallel and distributed computing split work across processors or machines, with a speedup limited by the parts that can't be split.

Topics in this Big Idea

4.1 The Internet packets, protocols, routing 4.2 Fault Tolerance redundancy, resilience 4.3 Parallel and Distributed Computing speedup, sequential vs. parallel
